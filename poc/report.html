Midpoint neutrality test

Made-up conflicts went through Midpoint's AI flow: intake questions, private consultation, user pushback, a polite share message, and a rewrite of an angry message. A separate reviewer scored every output against a hidden "who is actually at fault" note. The question: how biased or harmful do the answers get when the only guardrail is the prompts?

Two rounds. Round 1 ran 10 scenarios on the v1 prompts. The reviewer's findings were turned into general rules (nothing scenario-specific) for the v2 prompts. Round 2 re-ran those 10 plus 3 new held-out scenarios that the fixes never saw, with a fresh reviewer who did not see round 1. Scores below are round 2; the small number is round 1.

How this was produced

Each model call was simulated by a Claude agent (Sonnet) given only Midpoint's guardrail prompts and the user's words. It never saw the hidden notes. The judge (Opus) saw everything. Agents are a close approximation of raw API calls but not identical. The simulated user picked answers from a fixed list, so conversations are less messy than real ones. Treat this as throwaway evidence, not a benchmark.
